Pyrenoid Evolution Database — Classification Guide

How to classify what you see

A visual key for every category on the submission form, in the same order it appears there — read alongside a paper's figures to standardise how different people call the same features.

Classify what the image actually shows, not what you'd expect from the species. Imaging artefacts, growth-condition effects, and mis-speciation in older literature are real, but should be corrected post-hoc rather than guessed around at entry time. The diagrams below are original schematics, not reproductions of any published figure — illustrative, not a real specimen. This is a working draft: expect it to keep changing as edge cases turn up.

01

Chloroplast morphology

The shape of the chloroplast itself, independent of the pyrenoid — worth calling before anything pyrenoid-specific, since it sets context for what "central" or "bulged" even means in that cell.

Discoid / lens-shaped
Flattened, disc-like — as in most Rhodophyta.
Star-shaped (stellate)
Extensively lobed, radiating — e.g. Zygnema, Porphyridium, Rhodella.
Cup-shaped
Open, bowl-like cross-section — e.g. Chlamydomonas, Chlorella.
Two-lobed (butterfly)
Two lobes joined by a central/basal pyrenoid — e.g. Cryptomonas, Pavlova.
Lobed
Many small, irregular lobes — distinct from the broader radiating pattern of stellate.
Cuboid (axial plate)
Flat, plate-like.
Reticulate
Net-like, perforated.
Other
Includes ring-shaped and ribbon-shaped/helical — rare enough in TEM data that they're not worth separate categories. Describe in notes.
Pyrenoids per chloroplast: a plain count — most unicellular algae have one, but note how many are visible if more.
Edge cases not separately coded: a stellate chloroplast sharing one pyrenoid across its lobes via a shared cap, or multiple chloroplasts sharing one extra-chloroplastic cap — rare enough to describe in the notes field rather than force into a dropdown.
Cell type: only fill in if presence or form is cell-type specific (e.g. "gametes only") — leave blank otherwise.
Reference: Solymosi, K. — Plastid Structure, Diversification and Interconversions I. Algae.
02

General class

The pyrenoid's position relative to the chloroplast body.

Central
Not in contact with the chloroplast envelope — e.g. Chlamydomonas, Porphyridium, Thalassiosira.
Stalked
Bulges out through the envelope via a narrow constriction — apical or lateral.
Bulged
Same idea, but no constriction — wide-based from where it leaves the chloroplast.

Junctional
Sits at the junction where lobes meet — a constricted region. Butterfly/stellate chloroplasts.

Transverse
Centred along the chloroplast's long axis, but spans the full width along the short axis — touching the envelope on both sides. Distinct from central, which touches neither.
Other
Doesn't fit the options above — describe in the free-text field.
Position (stalked/bulged only): apical (at the end of the chloroplast) or lateral (from the side).
03

Matrix shape

The shape of the pyrenoid matrix itself, separate from the chloroplast or the general class above.

Spherical / pseudo-spherical
Broadly rounded — includes matrices that appear slightly misshapen only due to a stalked constriction.
Lenticular
Lens-shaped — e.g. diatom pyrenoids.
Lobed
One matrix, several rounded lobes.
Multinucleate
Fragmented into separate matrices, divided by traversing thylakoids — e.g. Anthoceros.
Amorphous
Continuous but not spherical and not clearly lobed.
Other
Describe in the notes field.
Diameter: estimate in µm from the image if a scale bar is present — even a rough figure is useful.
04

Thylakoids around the matrix

This is about proximity, not passage. Are thylakoids running close around the outside of the matrix? That's this section. Do membranes pass through the matrix itself? That's the next section, "Traversing membranes" — a genuinely separate question, and one pyrenoid can be a "yes" to both, one, or neither.

Naked (no)
No thylakoids running close around the boundary.
Present
Thylakoids run close around the matrix boundary.
05

Traversing membranes

Whether membranes penetrate through the matrix itself — the core call for the whole classification scheme. First: present/absent/unclear on the form; if present, these four shapes describe how.

Sheets
Continuous, unbroken sheets spanning the matrix — e.g. P. bulbiculosus (multiple sheets).
Tubulated
Appears fenestrated (perforated) in 2D section.
Curved
Follows a curved path — e.g. Porphyridium.
Invaginated
Folds inward from the boundary — usually can't resolve which membrane (envelope vs. other) at typical resolution, so just call it invagination.
Continuous with external thylakoid network? Only asked for sheets, tubulated, or curved — not invaginations, which by definition aren't continuous with the network.
Extent: complete (spans the whole matrix, usual for sheets/curved), partial (enters from the edge, doesn't reach the other side — usual for tubulated/fenestrations), or discontinuous.
Number of traversions: 1 / 2 / 3 / many — applies whether or not they fully pass through (e.g. Porphyridium's curved traversions, or multiple sheets as in P. bulbiculosus).
06

Diffusion barrier

Three independent yes/no/unclear questions, not mutually exclusive — a pyrenoid could plausibly have more than one of these at once.

Carbohydrate barrier

Present
Starch — also called capping vesicle / pyrenoid sac / pyrenoid cap in older TEMs.
Location: chloroplastic (usually central pyrenoids) or extra-chloroplastic/cytosolic (usually stalked/bulged — a membrane sits between starch and matrix).
Number of granules/plates: 1 / 2 / 3 / 4+, visible as concentric layers above.
Appearance: light or dark, depending on staining.
Coverage: complete, or partial — e.g. multiple granules that don't fully cover the surface, or a capping vesicle that structurally can't (extra-chloroplastic + bulging).

Shell

Present
Thin protein layer — often hard to observe; don't force a call if it's genuinely not resolvable.

Membrane barrier

Present
A distinct membrane boundary directly enclosing the matrix.
Origin: thylakoid / envelope / other.
Morphology: appressed (flat against the matrix) / curved / other.
Reference for the shell case: Holdsworth, R.H. (1968) — crystalline matrix boundary in a diatom pyrenoid.
07

Plastoglobuli

Small, roughly spherical lipid droplets, sometimes visible in or around the pyrenoid.

Present
Small dark globules — note the actual location seen (often peripheral).
08

Nucleomorph

A vestigial nucleus retained from a secondary endosymbiosis — present in some lineages (e.g. cryptophytes, chlorarachniophytes), never in others. Same yes/no + location pattern as plastoglobuli above.
